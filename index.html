SAGE

A Hybrid Vision–Language and Geometry Framework for Work-Zone Pathway Prediction under Temporary Traffic Control

Semantic-to-Action Grounded Execution

Jiaxi Liu, Keshu Wu*, Weizhe Tang, Bofeng Cao, Sikai Chen, Xiaopeng Li, Steven T. Parker, Bin Ran

University of Wisconsin–Madison

*Corresponding author

Paper arXiv Code ROADWork data Bird's-eye view

SAGE uses a vision–language model's trajectory as an opinion over intact geometric candidates and checks the chosen path against MUTCD provisions with a bounded intervention, so geometry writes every coordinate of the output.

Highlights

  1. Selection is the bottleneck. The 120 geometric candidates contain a path within 10 px of the human demonstration in 87% of episodes, yet selection regret accounts for 53% of the geometric ranker's error.

  2. Trajectory as opinion. SAGE-VLM's trajectory ranks the intact candidates by distance, and rank fusion with the geometric score improves all four tested generator–selector combinations.

  3. Read the zone, then check the path. MUTCD-structured supervision has SAGE-VLM state the lane status and hazard side before its decision; MUTCD-derived predicates drive a bounded intervention that cuts hard-constraint violations by 37%.

  4. Accurate with a faster VLM. SAGE lowers ADE by 8.3% and FDE by 12.0% relative to the geometric ranker, is comparable to OneVL in accuracy with a VLM that answers in 3.06 s against 3.75 s, and outperforms four other adapted driving VLMs in displacement error and safety measures.

How SAGE works

Read, decide, execute, check: four steps meet in one shared pool of candidate paths. Geometry builds every path; SAGE-VLM and the MUTCD predicates choose among them.

Read

State the work zone

SAGE-VLM first states the lane status and the hazard side, the side from which traffic is redirected.

reading + decision 0.73 s
Decide

Commit to a passage

It then gives a passage decision, a goal, and a 15-point trajectory, which SAGE uses only as an opinion.

goal 0.92 s · path 3.04 s
Execute

Fuse two rankings

Each of the 120 RD-YNet candidates gets a geometric rank and an opinion rank from its distance to the SAGE-VLM path.

60 + 60 candidates
Check

Apply MUTCD predicates

Z0 may replace a failing choice with the highest-ranked passing candidate within Δs ≤ 0.15 and D ≤ 38.4 px.

no trainable parameters

s(j) = 0.7 · ρ(geometric score) + 0.3 · ρ(distance to the SAGE-VLM path)  →  output: one of the 120 intact candidates

The provisions behind the checks

§6B.06 ¶02The work space is the portion of the highway closed to road users.RSI > 0: the path enters the restricted-space proxy.
§6B.05 ¶02Road users whose normal path must be redirected shall be directed to a new path with channelizing devices.CBC > 0: the path enters the channelization proxy.
§6L.06 ¶17, ¶19An arrow board in arrow or chevron mode is used only for lane closures; with the channelizing line it marks the closed side.The path ends on the hazard side stated in SAGE-VLM's reading.

The predicates are image-space proxies of the provisions; determining regulatory compliance requires a separate engineering assessment.

Watch SAGE decide

Four ROADWork validation frames, step by step. Every path, rank, reading, and check in the videos is a stored output of the evaluated system.

The bird's-eye view

Each path mapped onto the road with the paper's device-anchored metric frame: MUTCD drums (36 in, 0.91 m) calibrate the camera height and horizon of each recording with enough drums, the others use the median calibration, and a flat-ground pinhole model gives metres ahead and to the side.

Measures follow the paper's definitions. Lateral error compares each path with the demonstration at the same image row (the same distance ahead) within rows at least 80 px below the horizon. Lane tolerance holds when the maximum lateral error stays within 1.83 m, half of a 12-ft lane. Clearance is the gap between the 1.07-m half-width footprint of the AASHTO passenger car and a detected object at its ground-contact row; a value below −0.30 m counts as a strike.
